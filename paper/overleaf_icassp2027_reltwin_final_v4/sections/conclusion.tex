\section{Conclusion}
RelTwin targets a specific grounding failure: a real sound window can still be the wrong answer to a relational query. Co-occurring inverse relations create explicit local answer negatives, enabling a simple candidate loss on top of timestamp supervision. Our controlled comparisons distinguish this paired learning design from optional exchange and setwise extensions, and our diagnostics require correct answer changes rather than mere output diversity. Public system scores provide context; they do not replace the matched training evidence or establish natural relational generalization. The central contribution is a concrete supervision design and its audited behavioral evidence, not a claim of a new contrastive principle.
