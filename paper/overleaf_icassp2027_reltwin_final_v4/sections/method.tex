\section{Query-Specific Window Learning}
\subsection{Two true relations, two different answers}
Let $x$ be a recording, $q$ a query, and $G$ its target interval set. RelTwin reuses two ESC-50 excerpts~\cite{piczak2015esc}, $A$ and $B$, to form two windows $W_+=(A\!\rightarrow\!B)$ and $W_-=(B\!\rightarrow\!A)$ in the same recording. Each window spans the \emph{entire ordered sequence}, not only one constituent event. For the two inverse queries, the answer-label matrix is
\begin{equation}
Y=\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &W_+&W_-\\ \hline
q_+&1&0\\
q_-&0&1
\end{array}.
\label{eq:labels}
\end{equation}
In contrast, a label asking only whether each description occurs somewhere in $x$ would mark both queries true. Such labels do not themselves specify Eq.~\ref{eq:labels}; this is a difference in supervision, not an impossibility claim about globally pretrained models.

Each recording has two templates, ``followed by'' and ``and then,'' with both directions. Ordinary category queries target all occurrences of $A$ or $B$ for rehearsal. We alternate which relation appears earlier, controlling a simple constant-position preference. Excerpt reuse holds source material and event categories fixed within an inverse pair. The construction still uses repeated excerpts and silence; its layout-specific gap settings do not eliminate every synthetic shortcut.

\subsection{Contrast timestamp answers during training}
For the timestamp token sequence $y(S)$ describing interval set $S$, define its mean token log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
s_\theta(x,q,S)=\frac{1}{|y(S)|}\sum_t\log P_\theta(y_t\mid x,q,y_{<t}).
\end{equation}
Candidates are $C=(W_+,W_-)$. Their normalized preferences are $p_q(j)=\operatorname{softmax}_j(s_\theta(x,q,C_j)/\tau)$, not calibrated event-presence probabilities. The paired candidate loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm cand}=-\tfrac12[\log p_{q_+}(1)+\log p_{q_-}(2)].
\label{eq:candidate}
\end{equation}
For one query, this is the standard logistic loss $\log(1+\exp((s_- -s_+)/\tau))$. It explicitly compares the correct answer with the other \emph{present} window. We combine it with sequence supervision on both correct answers and category-query rehearsal:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm seq}+\lambda\mathcal L_{\rm cand}
+\beta\mathcal L_{\rm replay}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
SFT uses the same data, correct answers, and rehearsal, but omits $\mathcal L_{\rm cand}$. We refer to Eq.~\ref{eq:loss} as \emph{RelTwin-Cand}. It is the existing no-exchange configuration, not a newly tuned checkpoint. At test time the adapted model generates interval sets without access to $W_+$ or $W_-$.

\subsection{Testing whether extra objectives are needed}
RBEE adds $\lambda\JS(p_{q_+},\Pi p_{q_-})$, where $\Pi$ swaps the two candidate indices. This encourages exchange consistency, not architectural equivariance. Perfectly fitted candidate labels already satisfy the desired exchange, so this term supplies no additional optimal label.

SetPO is a subsequent 64-update stage with six answers: the two windows, their expanded versions, their union, and one interval spanning both. Soft targets use the mean of set-IoU and symmetric best-match interval F1; the latter is not one-to-one event F1. Its comparison is RBEE continued for the same 64 updates, not an untrained parent alone. These objectives are evaluated as optional extensions, not assumed sources of improvement.

\subsection{Check both localization and relation selection}
For interval unions, $\setiou(S,G)=|S\cap G|/|S\cup G|$. PairAcc requires both inverse-query predictions to achieve set-IoU $\geq0.5$. We additionally require each to overlap its own target strictly more than the opposite target, yielding JointPairAcc. Swap error means that at least one query violates this strict preference, including ties.

The distinction has a simple counterexample: for two disjoint equal-length windows, returning their union for \emph{both} queries gives IoU $0.5$ each and passes PairAcc, but fails JointPairAcc. This is a task-specific diagnostic, related to paired compositional evaluation~\cite{thrush2022winoground}, not a claim that standard IoU is useless or that paired accuracy is a new general metric.
