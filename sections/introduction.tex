\section{Introduction}
Audio temporal grounding maps a natural-language query to event intervals in a recording~\cite{xu2021grounding,munakata2025amr}. When sound categories repeat, recognition alone cannot determine which occurrence satisfies the query. A recording may contain both ``dog followed by rooster'' and ``rooster followed by dog,'' which inverse queries should map to different windows. Returning the same window for both is a query--window binding error: the prediction is acoustically supported but relationally incorrect.

The challenge is to distinguish two locally valid timestamp answers, not to construct an obviously incorrect negative. Positive sequence supervision raises the correct answer's likelihood without requiring it to outrank another acoustically supported window. A controlled contrast should hold the audio fixed, change the queried relation, and compare complete interval sets because errors can involve occurrence, coverage, count, or boundaries.

RelTwin (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}) introduces this competition through two-stage fine-tuning. Shared excerpts form opposite-order windows whose targets exchange under inverse queries. Stage~1 contrasts complete serialized answers and couples their preferences under reversal; Stage~2 compares interval sets with different coverage, counts, and boundaries. Timestamp-answer scores and localization replay adapt SpotSound-A and TimeAudio while preserving direct generation without candidate scoring at inference.

Both backbones improve on three public benchmarks and the 300-pair inverse-query evaluation. SpotSound-A candidate supervision reduces identical answers from 61 to 2; two-stage RelTwin reduces TimeAudio's from 160 to 34. A separate SpotSound-A intervention retains the candidate-supervision advantage after changing silence timing while preserving event waveforms, order, and queries.
