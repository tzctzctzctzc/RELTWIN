\section{Query-Specific Window Learning}
\subsection{Two true relations, two different answers}
RelTwin reuses two ESC-50 excerpts~\cite{piczak2015esc}, $A$ and $B$, to form windows $W_+=(A\!\rightarrow\!B)$ and $W_-=(B\!\rightarrow\!A)$ in one recording $x$. Each spans the \emph{entire ordered sequence}. For inverse queries $(q_+,q_-)$ and candidates $(W_+,W_-)$, the answer-label matrix is $Y=I_2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}). Both queries are present at recording level; the paired labels specify their local correspondence.

Each recording has two templates, ``followed by'' and ``and then,'' in both directions. Category queries target all occurrences of $A$ or $B$ for rehearsal. Alternating the earlier relation controls a constant-position preference; excerpt reuse fixes source material within a pair. Section~\ref{sec:timing} tests the contribution of layout-specific silence through a timing intervention.

\subsection{Contrast timestamp answers during training}
For the timestamp token sequence $y(S)$ describing interval set $S$, define its mean token log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
s_\theta(x,q,S)=\frac{1}{|y(S)|}\sum_t\log P_\theta(y_t\mid x,q,y_{<t}).
\end{equation}
Candidates are $C=(W_+,W_-)$. Their relative answer preferences are $p_q(j)=\operatorname{softmax}_j(s_\theta(x,q,C_j)/\tau)$. The paired candidate loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm cand}=-\tfrac12[\log p_{q_+}(1)+\log p_{q_-}(2)].
\label{eq:candidate}
\end{equation}
Reversing the query exchanges the positive timestamp sequence. Candidate cross-entropy makes this local discrimination explicit alongside correct-answer sequence supervision and category-query rehearsal:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm seq}+\lambda\mathcal L_{\rm cand}
+\beta\mathcal L_{\rm replay}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:loss} defines \emph{RelTwin}. SFT uses identical data and rehearsal with $\lambda=0$. At inference, the model generates timestamps from audio and a query, without candidates or an additional scorer.

\subsection{Joint localization and relation selection}
For interval unions, $\setiou(S,G)=|S\cap G|/|S\cup G|$. PairAcc requires both inverse-query predictions to achieve set-IoU $\geq0.5$. We additionally require each to overlap its own target strictly more than the opposite target, yielding JointPairAcc. Swap error means that at least one query violates this strict preference, including ties.

Returning the union of two disjoint equal-length targets for \emph{both} queries gives IoU $0.5$ each: PairAcc passes but JointPairAcc fails. Requiring a preference for the query's own target distinguishes relational selection from broad temporal coverage. Inspired by paired compositional evaluation~\cite{thrush2022winoground}, we report JointPairAcc alongside standard IoU to capture this behavior.
