\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
RelTwin organizes supervision around competition between locally valid timestamp answers (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}). Section~2.1 constructs query-dependent twins from shared excerpts and inverse queries. Section~2.2 uses complete-answer competition (Stage~1) to select the queried window and reverse the preference with the query. Section~2.3 introduces quality-aware interval-set preferences (Stage~2) for coverage, count, and boundary errors. For recording $x$ of duration $T$ and query $q$, the output is $S=\{[a_k,b_k]\}_{k=1}^{n}$, where $0\leq a_k<b_k\leq T$.

\subsection{Query-dependent twin construction}
This construction supplies the competing examples used by both training stages. To make the query determine window selection, we arrange the same ESC-50 excerpts~\cite{piczak2015esc}, $A$ and $B$, as $A\!\rightarrow\!B$ and $B\!\rightarrow\!A$ in one recording. Targets $W_\pm$ span each sequence, including its gap; queries $q_\pm$ request the respective orders. Both windows contain the same categories, but $W_-$ is negative for $q_+$ and positive for $q_-$. We alternate which order appears first.

\subsection{Stage 1: complete-answer competition}
Stage~1 establishes query-specific selection by comparing complete answers under each query. We serialize $S$ as comma-separated spans for SpotSound-A or temporal tokens for TimeAudio. Answer tokens $y(S)$ exclude the prompt and receive the mean log-probability
\begin{equation}
s_\theta(x,q,S)=\frac{1}{|y(S)|}\sum_t\log P_\theta(y_t\mid x,q,y_{<t}).
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
The $2\times2$ matrix $M_{ij}=s_\theta(x,q_i,W_j)$ indexes queries by rows and answers by columns, $i,j\in\{+,-\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}b). Its diagonal entries are the correct query--answer pairs. Sequence supervision minimizes $\mathcal L_{\rm seq}=-(M_{++}+M_{--})/2$, rewarding each correct answer independently. Candidate supervision adds the missing competition by training each diagonal score to exceed the other acoustically valid answer in its row. For $p_i(j)=\operatorname{softmax}_j(M_{ij}/\tau)$ with $\tau>0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm cand}=-\tfrac12[\log p_+(+)+\log p_-(-)].
\label{eq:candidate}
\end{equation}
Query reversal should also reverse the answer preference. We align the two distributions after exchanging candidate labels using the Jensen--Shannon divergence $\mathcal L_{\rm JS}=\operatorname{JS}(p_+,\Pi p_-)$, where $\Pi$ swaps the candidates. Replay retains ordinary localization supervision through examples $(x_r,q_r,S_r)$ with $\mathcal R_1=-s_\theta(x_r,q_r,S_r)$. Stage~1 optimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_1=\mathcal L_{\rm seq}+\lambda(\mathcal L_{\rm cand}
+\gamma\mathcal L_{\rm JS})+\beta\mathcal R_1.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 2: quality-aware interval-set preferences}
Stage~2 assigns graded preferences to imperfect answers according to coverage, interval count, and boundaries, rather than treating them as equally wrong. Its six candidates are $W_\pm$, their expanded versions, their enclosing span, and their union. Expansions expose boundary excess; the span includes intervening background; the union adds the competing occurrence.

For candidate $S_j$ and target $W_i$, a four-axis quality vector $h_{ij}$ measures set-IoU, threshold margin, count agreement, and boundary accuracy. The threshold axis averages soft F1 at $\eta=0.3/0.5$. Each interval's best-match IoU $u$ contributes $\max(0,u-\eta)/(1-\eta)$; averaging over predictions and targets gives soft precision and recall. With $n_S$ denoting interval count, count agreement is $1-|n_{S_j}-n_{W_i}|/\max(n_{S_j},n_{W_i},1)$. Boundary accuracy is $\exp(-e)$: $e$ averages the summed endpoint error for each target's best-IoU match, divided by $2\max(b-a,0.1)$ for target $[a,b]$.

Among $K$ candidates, let $d^+_{ij}$ and $d^-_{ij}$ count those dominated by and dominating $S_j$. Dominance means no worse on any axis and better on at least one. We define utility as
\begin{equation}
v_{ij}=\tfrac12\left[\tfrac14\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}h_{ij\ell}
+\frac{d^+_{ij}-d^-_{ij}}{K-1}\right].
\label{eq:quality}
\end{equation}
Mean quality preserves graded differences; dominance rewards improvements without sacrificing another axis. Soft targets $t_i=\operatorname{softmax}(v_i/\tau_q)$ assign higher probability to better interval sets. The same answer scores, normalized over all six candidates, learn this distribution through $\mathcal L_{\rm set}=-\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}t_i(j)\log p_i(j)$. The six-way permutation $\Pi=(2,1,4,3,5,6)$ swaps targets and expansions, fixing the span and union.

Replay candidates perturb boundaries, add/drop events, merge spans, split the longest interval, or cover the recording. The same quality rule defines their listwise loss $\mathcal L_{\rm set,r}$. On replay examples, the frozen base model supplies a reference distribution $p_0$; the KL constraint anchors preferences $p_r$ to this reference to help retain existing localization preferences. Stage~2 optimizes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_2&=\mathcal L_{\rm seq}+\lambda(\mathcal L_{\rm set}
+\gamma\mathcal L_{\rm JS})+\beta\mathcal R_2,\\
\mathcal R_2&=\mathcal R_1+\lambda\mathcal L_{\rm set,r}
+\kappa\operatorname{KL}(p_0\Vert p_r).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stage2}
\end{equation}

Both backbones share Eq.~\ref{eq:score}. Training updates LoRA parameters and, for TimeAudio, temporal/audio connectors. Inference remains direct timestamp generation from $(x,q)$, without twin construction or candidate scoring.
