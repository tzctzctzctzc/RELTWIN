\begin{abstract}
Audio temporal grounding must distinguish which event occurrence answers a query, even when opposite event orders coexist in one recording. Returning the same window for inverse queries reveals a query--window binding error. We introduce RelTwin, an add-on fine-tuning method that constructs opposite-order windows from shared excerpts and contrasts their timestamp answers. Both windows have acoustic support; the query determines the correct answer. RelTwin combines answer competition, exchange consistency, and set-level preferences while preserving direct timestamp generation. On SpotSound-Bench, Clotho-Moment, and UnAV-100, adapted SpotSound-A reaches 59.43, 86.68, and 74.03 mIoU; adapted TimeAudio exceeds published mIoU by 8.16, 5.48, and 14.48 points. Matched candidate-supervision experiments show 31.25 points of joint-localization gain on synthetic relation-development data and 1.02 points of SpotSound mIoU gain. The binding advantage persists under timing intervention, supporting supervision of which occurrence answers the query.
\end{abstract}
