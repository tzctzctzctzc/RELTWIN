\begin{abstract}
Audio temporal grounding requires identifying which event occurrence satisfies a query. When opposite event orders coexist, a model can localize a real sequence yet answer the wrong relation. We introduce RelTwin, a fine-tuning framework that contrasts locally valid timestamp answers. Shared excerpts form opposite-order windows in one recording; inverse queries exchange their target assignments. Candidate supervision and query-exchange consistency train relation-specific selection, followed by set-level preferences over coverage, count, and boundaries. Across SpotSound-Bench, Clotho-Moment, and UnAV-100, RelTwin improves both SpotSound-A and TimeAudio, with mIoU gains of up to 4.23 and 14.48 points over their published baselines. On controlled inverse-query development pairs, candidate supervision raises JointPairAcc by 31.25 percentage points over matched paired SFT. This advantage persists under altered silence timing, supporting improved query--window binding while inference retains direct timestamp generation.
\end{abstract}
