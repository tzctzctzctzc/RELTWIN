\begin{abstract}
Audio temporal grounding must select the event occurrence answering a query, even when opposite event orders coexist in one recording. Returning the same window for inverse queries reveals a query--window binding error. We introduce RelTwin: shared excerpts form opposite-order windows whose timestamp answers compete under each query. Both windows have acoustic support; the requested order determines the correct answer. Candidate supervision complements sequence learning while retaining direct timestamp generation. Against paired SFT, RelTwin improves joint localization by 31.25 points on a synthetic relation-development set and SpotSound mIoU by 1.02 points, with binding gains retained under timing intervention. Complementary system evaluations of NOVA with selective refinement reach 59.39, 86.86, and 73.46 mIoU on SpotSound, Clotho-Moment, and the released UnAV subset, respectively. Controlled training comparisons identify the contribution of query-conditioned answer competition.
\end{abstract}
