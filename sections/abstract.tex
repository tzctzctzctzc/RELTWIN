\begin{abstract}
Audio temporal grounding maps a text query to the corresponding event intervals. When the same sounds recur in different orders, event recognition alone cannot determine which occurrence satisfies a relational query: multiple windows are acoustically valid, yet only one matches the requested relation. We introduce RelTwin, a fine-tuning framework that contrasts such locally valid timestamp answers. Shared sound excerpts form opposite-order windows, and inverse queries exchange their correct targets. RelTwin first learns query-dependent occurrence selection through complete-answer competition, then ranks interval sets by coverage, count, and boundary accuracy. Across SpotSound-Bench, Clotho-Moment, and UnAV-100, it improves SpotSound-A and TimeAudio by up to 4.23 and 14.48 mIoU points, respectively. On 300 inverse-query pairs, SpotSound-A candidate supervision raises joint accuracy from 55.33 to 81.67; TimeAudio also improves over paired SFT. Separate silence interventions support SpotSound-A's selection gains. Inference retains direct timestamp generation.
\end{abstract}
