\subsection{Timestamp contrasts recover query-specific selection}
\input{tables/training_results}
The paired diagnostic directly tests whether reversing a query changes the selected event window (Table~\ref{tab:training}). Paired SFT returns identical answers for 37 of 160 inverse-query pairs, spanning 22 recordings; RelTwin reduces this count to one. Of those 37 pairs, 27 become jointly correct. This recovery connects answer differentiation to the requested relation, rather than merely to producing different timestamps. Figure~\ref{fig:pair} shows an example: RelTwin selects the corresponding window for each query, whereas SFT repeats one answer.

JointPairAcc increases by 31.25 points over paired SFT, with source-group 95\% CI $[22.35,40.50]$, while Swap error decreases by 32.50 points. Across all pairs, 51 change from incorrect to jointly correct, one changes in the opposite direction, and 24 remain incorrect. These transitions show that the aggregate gain is driven by recovering query-specific answers. Relation mIoU also increases by 13.97 points (CI $[9.84,17.51]$), connecting improved selection with temporal overlap. The example retains some boundary undercoverage, illustrating why relation selection and interval accuracy are measured separately.

The matched models share positive examples, rehearsal, initialization, and update count; RelTwin adds explicit competition between the two timestamp answers. Across its three training seeds, JointPairAcc averages 81.46 and identical-answer counts are 1, 0, and 0. Thus, the reduction in same-answer collapse is reproduced across the three RelTwin runs, alongside the primary paired-SFT comparison.
